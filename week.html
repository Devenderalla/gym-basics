Build my week

Three questions and you have a full training week — which days you train, what you do on each one, and which days you deliberately don't. Then just open it when you get to the gym.

What's your training level?

Beginner — new to the gym or still learning the basics. Intermediate — you know the basic exercises and train consistently. Advanced — substantial training experience.

How the week is built

Your level decides the split — beginners train the whole body each session because that is how movements get learned; intermediate and advanced weeks separate upper and lower, or push, pull and legs. Your goal changes the sets, reps and rest, and whether conditioning appears. Rest days are part of the plan, not what's left over. More training days is not automatically better, and the planner will say so if you pick more than your level needs.

Are you actually getting stronger?

The kg box tells you what you did last time, which is the question you have at the rack. This is the other one. One line per week per exercise, heaviest session of that week, and what changed since the week before — because overload is not only weight: a week that added two reps at the same load progressed, and is counted as progress here. Three weeks with nothing added is called what it is.

What you lifted

Every weight you put in the kg box on a workout row is kept on this device, per exercise. Download it as a spreadsheet whenever you want it somewhere else — or somewhere safer than one phone. One row per exercise per day: the date, the weight, the sets and reps, and how it felt.

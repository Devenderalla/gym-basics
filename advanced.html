The structure

A five-day week

Upper/lower strength days carry the heavy work early in the week; the pump days add volume when you're already fatigued. The lower-strength day is written out in full below.

Day 1–2 · Strength

Lower / Upper · 5–8 reps
  • Back squat or deadlift 4 × 5–8
  • Bench or overhead press 4 × 5–8
  • Barbell row / pull-up 4 × 6–10
  • Long rests, no techniques 3–4 min

Day 4–5 · Volume

Lower / Upper · 8–15 reps
  • Machine + dumbbell variations 3–4 × 8–15
  • Intensity techniques live here 1–2 / session
  • Shorter rests 60–90s
  • Isolation finishers drop sets

Day 6 · Optional

Conditioning · weak points
  • Assault bike or rower intervals 15–20 min
  • SkiErg + carries circuit 3 rounds
  • One lagging muscle, 4–6 sets your pick
  • Days 3 & 7: rest non-negotiable

Intensity techniques

Six ways to make a set harder without adding weight. Each earns its place on machines and isolation work — none belong on a heavy barbell set.

Drop set

Reach near-failure, strip ~30% of the weight, continue without rest. One or two drops maximum.

Best on: machines, cables, dumbbell isolation

Superset

Two exercises back-to-back, then rest. Opposing muscles (curl + pushdown) or same muscle for a deeper burn (curl + hammer curl).

Best on: accessories · saves 10+ min

Rest-pause

Near-failure, rack it, breathe 15–20 seconds, squeeze out 3–5 more. Repeat once. One set becomes three mini-sets.

Best on: machine presses, calf raises

Tempo training

Assign the rep a clock — 4 seconds down, 1 up. Less weight, more tension, better positions. The most underrated tool here.

Best on: everything, periodically

Paused reps

A hard 2-second stop at the weakest point — the chest on a press, the hole of a squat. Kills bounce, builds honesty.

Best on: squat, bench, RDL — moderate loads

Mechanical drop set

Same weight, easier variation when you fail: incline press → flat press, wide pulldown → underhand. The load stays, the leverage changes.

Best on: pulldowns, presses, raises

The advanced rule of thumb

Heavy compound work stays clean — straight sets, long rests, a rep in reserve. Techniques live on the last set of accessory exercises, one or two per session. If everything is intense, nothing is.

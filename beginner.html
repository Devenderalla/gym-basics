Beginner
full-body session

One complete workout you can repeat two or three times a week for your first month. Machines only, back supported, nothing to balance — so all your attention goes to learning the movements.

Pre-gym

Before you leave home

The session starts before the gym does. None of this is complicated — it's just the difference between arriving ready and arriving flustered.

·Eat something lightA banana, toast, or curd-and-fruit about an hour before. Training on a heavy stomach is miserable; training completely empty makes some people dizzy.Energy60–90 min before
·Drink water through the dayArrive hydrated instead of chugging a bottle at the door.HydrationAll day
·Know today's planIt's this page. Six exercises, in order. Screenshot the list if it helps.Confidence2 min
·Pack the kitClosed shoes, clothes you can move in, a water bottle, a small towel. That's the whole list.KitSee what to bring
MindsetYour only job today is to learn where things are and how they feel. The weights are a detail. Nobody is watching you, and the ones who glance had a first day too.
02

Warm-up

5–10 min

Warm muscles learn faster and complain less. Easy cardio first, then a minute of loosening the joints you're about to use.

01Treadmill walk5 km/h, no incline. Clip on the red safety key.Whole body5 min
02Arm circles + shoulder rollsTen each direction, standing anywhere out of the way.Shoulders1 min
03Hip circles + bodyweight squatsTen slow squats to a comfortable depth, hands out in front for balance.Hips, knees2 min
Form cueYou should finish the warm-up slightly warmer and slightly looser — not out of breath. Save the effort for the machines.
03

Main workout

~25 min

Six machines, in an order that alternates legs, pushes and pulls so nothing tires out early. Two sets of each, 8–10 controlled repetitions, about 90 seconds of rest between sets. Use the lightest setting that makes the last two reps feel like work.

01Leg pressFeet shoulder-width, mid-plate. Stop before your lower back peels off the seat.Quads, glutes2 × 8–10rest 90s
02Chest pressHandles lined up with mid-chest — adjust the seat first, every time.Chest, triceps2 × 8–10rest 90s
03Lat pulldownBar to your collarbone, never behind your neck. Let it rise slowly.Back, biceps2 × 8–10rest 90s
04Seated cable rowPull to your stomach, elbows close, torso still. Proud chest at the end.Mid-back, biceps2 × 8–10rest 90s
05Leg curlRoller just above the heel; curl in smoothly, resist on the way back.Hamstrings2 × 10–12rest 60s
06Dumbbell shoulder pressThe lightest pair on the rack, seated with full back support.Shoulders2 × 8–10rest 90s
The one ruleIf a weight doesn't move smoothly for every listed rep, drop it one setting. Going lighter is not a failure — it's the actual technique.
Common mistakeCopying the person next to you. They're on week two hundred of their plan; you're on day one of yours. Your numbers are supposed to look different.

Cool-down

~5 min
01Easy bike or walkA pace where your breathing settles back to normal.Recovery3–5 min
02Gentle stretchesThighs, chest, back — 30 seconds each, stretch to mild tension, never pain, no bouncing.Trained muscles3 min
ThenWipe down anything you sweated on, put the dumbbells back, and go home. That's a complete session: legs, a push, a pull, core, and a warm-up at each end.

Recovery

Until next session

Muscles grow between sessions, not during them. The unglamorous checklist:

·Eat a normal meal with proteinDal, eggs, paneer, chicken, curd — within a couple of hours. Nothing special to buy.RebuildingWithin ~2h
·Keep drinking waterEspecially the first weeks, when soreness is at its loudest.HydrationRest of day
·SleepThe single most effective recovery tool, and it's free.Everything7–9h
·Expect soreness — a day or two of itNormal, harmless, and it fades fast as your body adapts. Sharp pain during exercise is different: stop, and ask staff.Expectation48h
ScheduleRepeat this session with at least one rest day between — Monday / Wednesday / Friday is the classic. Twice a week also works and still builds real progress.
The roadmap

Your first 4 weeks

Same session, evolving focus. Progress in month one is learning — the weights follow on their own.

Week 1

Learn the room

Find every machine, set every seat, use the lightest settings. Success this week is simply turning up twice and finishing.

Week 2

Improve technique

Same weights, better reps: slower lowering, full range, breathing on rhythm. Ask staff to watch one exercise per visit.

Week 3

Add volume

Take each exercise from 2 sets to 3. The session grows to about 50 minutes. Weights stay where they are.

Week 4

Start overloading

Where all 3 sets feel smooth, move the pin one plate down or take the next dumbbell up. That small jump, repeated for months, is the entire secret.

After week 4?

Keep running this session and adding small amounts of weight for as long as it keeps working — that's often two or three months. When machine exercises feel routine and you're curious about dumbbells, the intermediate program is your next step. There is no prize for rushing there.

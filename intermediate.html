The structure

Three days, one rotation

Push day is written out in full below as the guided session. Pull and legs follow the same six-phase spine — swap in the exercises from their cards.

Push

Chest · shoulders · triceps
  • Dumbbell bench press 3 × 8–12
  • Incline dumbbell press 3 × 8–12
  • Machine shoulder press 3 × 8–12
  • Cable fly 3 × 10–15
  • Lateral raise 3 × 12–15
  • Triceps pushdown 3 × 10–15

Pull

Back · rear delts · biceps
  • Lat pulldown 3 × 8–12
  • Seated cable row 3 × 8–12
  • One-arm dumbbell row 3 × 8–12
  • Face pull 3 × 12–15
  • Barbell curl 3 × 8–12
  • Dumbbell curl 2 × 10–15

Legs

Quads · glutes · hamstrings
  • Goblet squat 3 × 8–12
  • Romanian deadlift 3 × 8–12
  • Leg press 3 × 10–15
  • Walking lunge 3 × 8–10 / leg
  • Leg curl 3 × 10–15
  • Calf raise 3 × 10–15

How to schedule it

3 days a week: push / pull / legs with a rest day between each — the classic. 4 days: run the rotation continuously (push, pull, rest, legs, push…), so each session type comes around every 5–6 days. Prefer two sessions a week? Use an upper / lower pairing instead: combine push + pull into one upper day, keep the leg day as written.

Main workout — push day

~35 min

Compounds first while you're fresh, isolation after. Rest 90 seconds on the big presses, 45–60 on the isolation work. Every set stops one or two reps short of failure — the last rep should be hard, not ugly.

Exercise orderThe order is the program: big pressing while fresh, shoulders before they're cooked, isolation last. Swapping the pushdowns to first doesn't make them harder — it makes the presses worse.
Common mistakeAdding weight before finishing the rep range. Hit the top of the range on every set first — then add the smallest plate in the gym, drop back to the bottom of the range, and climb again.

The engine

Progressive overload

The difference between exercising and training is a notebook.

  1. Work inside the rep range. 3 × 8–12 means: pick a weight where you can just get 8s.
  2. Add reps session to session. 8, 8, 8 becomes 10, 9, 8 becomes 12, 11, 10.
  3. Top of the range on every set? Add the smallest jump available — 2.5 kg, one pin, the next dumbbell.
  4. Drop back to the bottom of the range and climb again. That loop, repeated for months, is the entire method.
  5. Stalled three sessions running? Check sleep and food first, then take the lighter week. Stalls are information, not failure.

When are you "advanced"?

Not at a number on the bar — when you've run structured training long enough that easy progress has stopped, you're comfortable with barbell squats, deadlifts and presses, and recovery is the limiting factor. Then the advanced program's intensity techniques start earning their keep.

Everything they never label

The first-day guide

Walk into a gym knowing what everything does. The floor map, the weights explained, your first 30 minutes planned, and the eight unwritten rules everyone else learned by watching.

Floor map How weights work The 12 machines First 30 minutes Etiquette What to bring

The doors are usually glass. You can see the whole floor from the street: machines with cables and pins and levers, people who clearly know what they're doing, and not one label explaining what any of it is for.

That's the actual barrier — not fitness. A gym is one of the only public places where everyone is expected to already know how the equipment works, and nothing tells you. This page describes the room before you have to stand in it.

How the weights work

Weight stacks
On the seated machines: a column of numbered rectangular plates with a pin. Put the pin under the number you want — lower number, lighter weight. Start with the pin near the top.
Barbells and plates
The long bar in the rack weighs 20 kg on its own, and lifting only the bar is a normal starting point, not a beginner's embarrassment. Round plates slide onto the ends.
Dumbbells
Sold in pairs by weight, racked lightest to heaviest. Start at 2–4 kg to learn a movement.

How much should I use?

The lightest weight that lets you do 8–10 repetitions with control, where the last two feel like work but your form doesn't change. If you can't finish the set smoothly, it's too heavy. Going lighter is not a failure — it's the actual technique.

The 12 machines you'll actually use

How to spot each one on the floor. Every name links to its card in the equipment library — photo, instructions, level-by-level starting points, and the mistake to avoid.

  1. Treadmill — the row of belted walking machines, usually facing a wall or a window. Start here: 5 minutes of walking is a complete warm-up.
  2. Stationary bike — seated bikes near the treadmills; the ones with a chair back are called recumbent. The gentlest thing in the building.
  3. Elliptical — the upright machine with two long foot pedals and handles that swing. No impact at all.
  4. Leg press — a large angled seat with a wide footplate up above you. The biggest muscles you have, back fully supported.
  5. Chest press — a seat with two handles at chest height that you push away from you.
  6. Lat pulldown — a seat with thigh pads and a long bar hanging overhead. Bar to your collarbone, never behind your neck.
  7. Seated cable row — a low seat with a footplate and a handle on a cable in front of you. The pulling counterpart to the chest press.
  8. Leg curl and extension — two similar seated machines with a padded roller near your ankles. One works the front of the thigh, one the back.
  9. Dumbbells — the long rack of pairs along a wall, facing mirrors. The mirrors are for checking form, not for being looked at.
  10. Cable machine — two tall towers with pulleys you slide up and down, and a bin of handles nearby. One machine that becomes many.
  11. Bench — the padded bench, often with a backrest that pins to different angles. The base for most dumbbell work.
  12. Squat rack — the tall metal cage at the back with a barbell in it. The most intimidating station in the gym, and one of the simplest.

Your first 30 minutes

It uses only the machines above, and it ends.

  1. Treadmill — 5 minutes. Walk at 5 km/h. This is your warm-up.
  2. Leg press — 2 sets of 10. Lightest plate. Rest 90 seconds between sets.
  3. Chest press — 2 sets of 10. Rest 90 seconds.
  4. Lat pulldown — 2 sets of 10. Rest 90 seconds.
  5. Seated row — 2 sets of 10. Rest 90 seconds.
  6. Bike — 5 minutes, easy. Cool down.
  7. Go home.

That's a complete session: legs, a push, a pull, and a warm-up at each end. If a weight doesn't move smoothly for all 10, drop it — that's the technique working, not failing.

The rules everyone else learned by watching

How to not look lost

  • Is this machine free? If there's no towel, bottle or bag on it and nobody standing nearby, it's free. If you're unsure, the whole sentence is: "Are you using this?"
  • Someone's on the machine you want. Say "Mind if I work in?" — it means taking turns between their sets. It's normal and expected.
  • Wipe it down after you use it. The spray and paper are on the wall.
  • Put the weights back. More noticed than anything you actually lift.
  • Nobody is watching you. Between sets, people look at their phone or at themselves. The room is full of people thinking about their own set.
  • You are allowed to ask the staff. Showing you how a machine works is literally their job, and asking on day one is the most normal thing in the building.
  • Headphones are a valid "not now" sign — yours and everybody else's.
  • Don't stand right in front of the dumbbell rack. Take the pair a step back so others can reach.
The whole list

What to wear, what to bring

Closed shoes with a reasonably flat sole. Clothes you can move in. A water bottle and a small towel.

That's the list. No gloves, no belt, no special kit, nothing to buy first.
